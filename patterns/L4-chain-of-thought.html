01 · The Idea

What is Chain-of-Thought?

Models blurt the first answer that comes to mind. Chain-of-Thought makes them show their work first — and writing the steps is what makes the answer right.

04 · Worked Example

One real case.

User asks
"A crew of 4 painters each finish 3 rooms a day. They work 5 days. The building has 70 rooms. How many are left unpainted?"
1
Pull out the numbers
4 painters, 3 rooms each per day, 5 days, 70 rooms total. Naming the parts before computing is the whole point of reasoning first.
2
Step one — per day
How many rooms get painted in a single day? 4 painters × 3 rooms = 12 rooms / day
3
Step two — over five days
Carry the previous result forward, don't restart. 12 rooms/day × 5 days = 60 rooms painted
4
Step three — what's left
Subtract painted from the total. 70 rooms − 60 painted = 10 left
→ Answer returned to user
"10 rooms are left unpainted." The answer is just the last step, and every step before it was easy to check. (Asked cold, with no steps, models often blurt a wrong number here.)
05 · Code Sketch

The shape in code.

Hover any line. The whole pattern is one change to the prompt.
# the entire trick: ask for the reasoning before the answer
prompt = question + "\nLet's think step by step."
 
# the model now writes its steps, then lands on an answer
response = model(prompt)
 
# the reasoning is everything above; the answer is the last line
answer = response.splitlines()[-1]
return answer, response
