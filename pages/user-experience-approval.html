Approval gates

Before the AI does something the user would not want to undo, it stops and shows what it is about to do. The user can say yes, edit, or say no.

01What is it?

A planned pause.

An approval step is a pause before an irreversible action: the AI shows exactly what it is about to do and waits for the user to confirm, edit, or cancel.

Show, don't summarise. The pause is only useful if the screen tells the truth about the action: who, how many, what is attached, what becomes public. Vague confirmations train people to click yes without reading.
03Where It Fits

Where it sits.

Two halves
The runtime that holds the action mid-air (the orchestration side) and the screen that shows it and collects the answer (the user interface side). This page is about the screen.
Pairs with
External action and tool use. The pause exists because the next step changes the world: a payment, an email, a deletion, a public post.
Not the same as
An "are you sure?" pop-up over everything. Pause on the few big ones; let the small ones flow. Overuse trains people to click through without reading.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • The action is hard or embarrassing to take back: money moved, messages sent, records changed, files deleted.
  • The user wants visible control. Even a fast confirm builds trust that the AI is not running off on its own.
  • Rules require a human signature: finance, legal, health, anything customer-facing at scale.
