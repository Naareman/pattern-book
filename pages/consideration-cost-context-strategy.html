Context-strategy tradeoff

Stuff the whole library, retrieve a few pages, or do a bit of both. Each choice trades cost and speed for coverage.

01What It Is

Three ways to feed the model what it needs to know.

The context strategy is the decision of how much external text rides along with the question. Pack the whole corpus into a long context window: simple, costly, often slow. Retrieve only the relevant pages: cheap and fast, but a miss in retrieval is a miss in the answer. Mix the two: retrieve generously and let a long context handle the rest. The right pick depends on how big the corpus is, how often it changes, and how much you can spend per call.

03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

Sits next to

Builds on
Knowing what costs what. The choice usually swings on the cost per question once you can see it for each option.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • The knowledge base is large enough that paying to pack it all into every prompt would blow the budget.
  • Answers depend on niche or recent information, so the choice between long context and retrieval changes quality.
  • The team is debating long-context models versus retrieval pipelines, and the call has to be defended with real numbers.
