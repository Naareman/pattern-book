Step-Back Prompting

Ask the model a broader question before the specific one so it retrieves the right concepts first.

01What It Is

Zoom out, then zoom in.

With Step-Back prompting, you instruct the model to first answer a more general, abstract question about the topic before tackling the original specific question. That abstraction step activates the relevant background knowledge and principles in context, making the subsequent specific answer more accurate. Think of it as asking "what field does this belong to and what are its core rules?" before asking the original question.

02An Example

Physics question, two steps.

Step-back question (you send this first)
What physics principles govern how temperature affects the resistance of a conductor?

The model recalls the relevant principles (Ohm's law, thermal motion of electrons, resistivity formula) and surfaces them before touching the original problem. This primes the context with the right concepts.

Original specific question (sent after)
A copper wire at 20 C has resistance 5 ohms. What is its resistance at 80 C?

Now grounded in the correct principles, the model applies the temperature coefficient formula accurately instead of guessing.

03Where It Fits

Its place in the taxonomy.

L4
Home
Single-Call Control, Cognitive Strategy sub-axis, in-prompt tier. The step-back instruction is text in the prompt that the model follows within one inference pass.
L4
Neighbor
Least-to-Most also decomposes before answering, but it breaks a hard question into easier sub-questions. Step-Back abstracts upward to a broader principle rather than breaking down.
Filing rule: In-prompt Cognitive Strategy. The abstraction step happens inside the model's reasoning trace, not across separate API calls or an orchestration layer.
04When It Matters

Who needs this.

Step-Back is most useful when the model gives plausible-sounding but factually wrong answers to domain-specific questions. It works well in science, law, and medicine where the wrong framework produces a confident wrong answer. If your model often answers the right question with the wrong underlying reasoning, a step-back question is a lightweight fix.
