Multi-Agent Debate

Two or more AI models argue opposing positions across explicit rounds of critique; a separate judge reads the whole debate and synthesizes one final answer.

Default in 2026Three vendors (Claude + GPT + Gemini), adversarial rounds with explicit critique, and a separate judge that synthesizes. Use when correctness benefits from adversarial pressure: legal review, code review, fact-checking. NOT a Council: a Council is collaborative peer review, Debate is attack and defend.
01The Idea

What is it?

A Multi-Agent Debate pits two or more models against each other on the same question. Each round, they read each other's positions and write back the strongest critique they can. A separate judge model reads the whole exchange and writes the final answer, citing whichever side held up.

02See It Work

Watch two models attack each other.

A real legal-review style question. Two models start on opposite sides. Round 2, they read each other and try to break the other's argument. Then a separate judge model picks the winning points.

Question (sent to both models)
Is this non-compete clause enforceable in California? "Employee shall not work for any competitor for 24 months post-termination."
1Round 1 · opening positions
A Claude Defending: unenforceable
California Business and Professions Code §16600 voids contracts that restrain anyone from engaging in a lawful profession. A blanket 24-month non-compete falls squarely in that prohibition.
B GPT Defending: enforceable
The narrow trade-secret exception under Edwards v. Arthur Andersen still permits restraints reasonably tailored to protect trade secrets. If this employee had access, a 24-month restraint may survive scrutiny.
2Round 2 · critiques (each model reads the other)
A Claude Attacks B
Critique of GPT Edwards explicitly rejected the "narrow restraint" reading the dissent argued for. AB 1076 (2024) codified that all post-employment non-competes are void, regardless of trade-secret framing. The exception you cite does not exist for non-competes, only for sale-of-business.
B GPT Attacks A
Critique of Claude Conceding: AB 1076 closes my carve-out. However, a properly-pleaded trade-secret misappropriation claim under CUTSA is still actionable. The clause is void, but the conduct it tries to prevent may still be restrainable through a different cause of action.
⚖Judge (separate model, reads both rounds)
The clause as written is unenforceable in California. Claude's citation to §16600 and AB 1076 (2024) is correct and was conceded by GPT in round 2. GPT's surviving point is also right and worth carrying forward: trade-secret protection in California is via CUTSA misappropriation claims, not through non-compete clauses.
Judge's job: weigh the rounds, keep the points that survived attack, discard the rest.

05Patterns It Uses

What's underneath.

Coordination: explicit round structure tells each model when to argue and when to attack Ensembling: multiple model calls (often different vendors) instead of just one Verification: each round is a critique pass that filters out weak claims Cascading: a separate judge model reads the whole debate and synthesizes
06When To Use

Pick this over the look-alikes.

Pick Debate over
LLM Council
Debate is adversarial: models try to break each other's argument. Council is collaborative peer review: models read each other and refine. Use Debate when you need attack pressure, Council when you need calibration.
Pick Debate over
Self-Consistency
Self-Consistency runs one model multiple times and votes. Debate runs different models attacking each other. Self-Consistency catches reasoning variance. Debate catches model blind spots.
Pick Debate over
Generator-Critic pair
A Generator-Critic is one round: critic gives feedback, generator revises, done. Debate is multi-round symmetric: both sides write, both sides attack, judge picks the winner. Use Debate when the cheaper one-shot critique already failed.
