Decide who can see and do what

Each role inside a customer carries its own short list of allowed actions. The assistant runs as the asking user, so it can only reach what they could already reach.

01What is it?

What is it?

Access control is the gate inside one customer: a sales rep, a manager, and the finance team all have different lists of what they may see and do. The AI assistant runs on behalf of whoever asked, carrying their identity through every call. Each search and each action then runs against their permissions, so the answer is filtered to what that person was already allowed to know.

The assistant inherits the asker's permissions. A request that a person could not make at the keyboard is also a request the AI cannot make on their behalf.
03Where it fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it covers: rules per role inside one customer. Reads, edits, and tool calls are all subject to the same gate.
Sits next to: isolation seals one customer from another. Access control sets the rules among the people inside that customer. Audit trails (the next page) record every allow and every deny.
Quick test: ask the same question through three different employee accounts. Do you get three different answers, each shaped by what that person may see? That is access control at work.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • Employees with different jobs share one assistant: an analyst, a manager, and a director should not see the same numbers.
  • The assistant can perform real actions (issue a refund, send an email, change a record) and the action should follow the asker's authority.
  • You handle data with mixed sensitivity inside one customer: HR records, board memos, salary tables, customer contracts.
