Application Patterns

Every AI feature is defined by what it does to its input. There are six kinds of jobs that cover all of them.

01The Idea

What is it?

One model, six jobs. What changes across AI features is not the model but the relationship between the input you give it and the output you want back. The same paragraph can be answered, shortened, sorted, picked apart, or turned into something new. Knowing which of these six jobs you are doing is the first thing to decide.

03A Real Example

Where each one lives.

A customer support chatbot answers "where is my order?"
Tap to see the job
Retrieval and Q&A. The app finds the answer in live order data and returns it. The output already existed somewhere; the AI located it.
A news reader turns a 1,200-word article into a 3-sentence brief
Tap to see the job
Transformation. The meaning is preserved, the form changes. Summaries, translations, and rewrites all live here.
A legal contract tool pulls dates, party names, and amounts from a 40-page PDF
Tap to see the job
Extraction and classification. The output is structured data drawn out of unstructured text, plus a label for each clause type.
A marketing tool writes three ad variants from a one-line product brief
Tap to see the job
Generation. The output did not exist in the input. The AI made it up from a tiny seed.
A product recommender scores ten items and surfaces the top three for a shopper
Tap to see the job
Decision and recommendation. The output is a pick or a ranking chosen from a defined set, not a free-form answer.
An HR onboarding bot creates the account, sends the welcome email, and assigns the training queue
Tap to see the job
Automation and workflow execution. The output is the running of a real procedure end to end, with side effects in the world.
04Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

The price is not one number. Each kind of job has a different shape of risk. The three axes below do not share a scale, so read each column on its own.

Easy to checkwas the answer right?
Creative workdid the AI invent something?
Risk if wrongwhat does a mistake cost?
Retrieval and Q&A
Transformation
Extraction and classification
Generation
Decision and recommendation
Automation and workflow execution

Extraction is the easiest to test and the safest to ship. Generation is the hardest to check because the answer is new every time. Automation carries the highest cost when wrong because the mistake is already done.
