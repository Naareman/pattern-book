Verification by another model

One model writes the answer. A second one acts as a judge and rates it. Often called LLM-as-Judge (LLM = large language model). The judge gives a score, a verdict, or a written critique.

01What is it?

Second pair of eyes.

After the first model answers, send the question and the answer to a second model with a clear rubric. The judge returns a verdict (good, bad), a score (1 to 5), or a short critique. You use that result to keep the answer, fix it, or escalate.

02An Example

Writer, then judge.

Question
Write a one-sentence refund policy for a small online store.
1
Writer model says: "We will refund any item within 30 days, no questions asked."
2
Judge model rubric: Is it clear? Is it specific? Does it cover edge cases like shipping fees?
Judge: 4/5. Clear and short. Ship as is.
Judge: 2/5. Missing shipping fee rule. Send back to the writer with that note.
The judge is just another call. Same model, different model, or a fine-tuned scorer. The rubric is the part that does the work.
03Where it fits

How it sits with nearby ideas.

Compare to
Asking the same model to double-check its own answer in one call is a single-call trick. Verification by another model uses a separate call (and often a separate model), so the check is independent.
Pairs with
Often paired with generator-critic loops (judge then revise) or with offline evaluation, where the same judge rates thousands of samples to compare two prompts or models.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • Quality matters and you can afford a second call before shipping.
  • Right and wrong are hard to define by simple rules (style, tone, helpfulness).
  • You want a cheap, repeatable score you can trend over time on a fixed test set.
