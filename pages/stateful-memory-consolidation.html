Memory consolidation

When the store fills up, the older entries get squeezed into shorter summaries that still hold the point.

01What it is

Many small notes turn into one short summary.

Consolidation periodically replaces a group of older memory entries on a topic with one shorter summary, keeping the point and dropping the detail so the store stays small enough for the model to load.

02An example

Five notes about coffee, squeezed to one.

/memory/events/ 5 entries · 412 tokens
3 JanSaid hates burnt taste, will not drink dark roast.
17 JanOrdered an oat-milk flat white again.
02 FebAsked for cafes with single-origin beans near home.
28 FebBought a small grinder, started making coffee at home.
11 MarSaid morning coffee only, decaf after 3pm.
/memory/summaries/coffee.md 1 entry · 38 tokens
Coffee, Jan to MarPrefers light-roast single-origin, oat-milk flat white, makes it at home, decaf after 3pm.
Before: five separate moments that each carry a small fact about how you like coffee.
03Where it fits

How it sits next to nearby ideas.

Sits next to
Dropping entries. Shrinking keeps the gist of a group of notes. Dropping throws a note away entirely. Most systems do both.
What it costs
You lose the original wording and the exact dates. The summary is the model's read of the group, not a quote, so it can blur or get something slightly wrong.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The memory store keeps growing and you cannot load all of it into a single answer anymore.
  • Many small notes are saying the same kind of thing and the model only needs the overall pattern.
  • You want a long-running profile that stays small even after months of use.
