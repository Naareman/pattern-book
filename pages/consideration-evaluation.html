Consideration

Evaluation

Checking that the AI is actually right, not just sounding sure of itself.

In plain words

An AI can give a wrong answer in a very confident voice. Evaluation means testing it on lots of questions where we already know the right answer, then counting how often it gets them right. If you do not test it, you are just hoping.

Why it matters

What goes wrong if you ignore it

A team ships a chatbot that looks great in a quick demo, so they skip real testing. In the wild it confidently invents fake refund rules and made-up product specs. Nobody notices for weeks because there were no checks in place, and by then thousands of customers got wrong answers.

Where it shows up most

Application patterns

Rough editorial estimate of where this matters most, not a measured score. Evaluation matters everywhere, but it bites hardest where outputs are combined, where models are trained or tuned, and where you shape a single answer.

Examples

Spam filters are measured on precision and recall: how much real spam they catch versus how often they block a good email by mistake.
Coding assistants are tested on benchmark sets of programming problems to see what share they solve correctly.
Translation tools are scored against human reference translations to track quality over time.
