LLM Council

Three top models from different vendors answer the same question, anonymously score each other, and one chairman synthesizes the final reply.

Default in 2026

Claude Opus 4.7 + GPT-5 + Gemini 2.5 Pro panel. Anonymize before peer review. Chairman = strongest model. Use only when correctness is worth roughly 3x the cost and 10s of added latency.

01The Idea

What is it?

Ask three different models the same hard question, let them peer-review each other anonymously, then have one chairman model write the final answer using what the council agreed on.

02See It Work

The council, live.

Tap Next to walk through one council deliberation: question goes out to three anonymous models in parallel, each scores the others' answers without knowing who wrote them, then a chairman synthesizes one final reply.

Question
Should a Series A SaaS company file for a US patent on its core algorithm, given competitors and 18 months of runway?
1Parallel answers
2Anonymous peer review
3Chairman synthesis
MODEL-A waiting
Claude Opus 4.7
Yes, but narrowly. File a provisional now ($300, locks priority), invest in a full filing only after Series B. The defensive value matters more than the offensive use at this stage.
B: 8/10 C: 7/10
MODEL-B waiting
GPT-5
No. At Series A, cash and speed matter more than IP. Patents take 2+ years to grant, your algorithm will have evolved, and trade-secret protection plus shipping fast gives you more durable advantage.
A: 7/10 C: 6/10
MODEL-C waiting
Gemini 2.5 Pro
It depends on whether your algorithm is genuinely novel. If yes, file a provisional ($300, 12-month window). If it's an obvious application of known methods, skip it: a denied patent reveals your method publicly.
A: 9/10 B: 6/10
Chairman Claude Opus 4.7
File a provisional patent ($300) within 30 days. Two of three council members agreed this captures defensive value cheaply without overcommitting cash. The dissent (Model B) is right that a full filing is premature, so do not pursue one until Series B. The condition Model C raised about genuine novelty applies: if the algorithm is an obvious extension of public work, skip the filing.
Agreement: 2 of 3 Cost: $0.42 + $0.31 + $0.28 + $0.39 = $1.40 per query Latency: 11.3 s

04The Price You Pay

What it costs.

Operational complexity
Vendor lock-in (lower is better)
Answer quality on hard questions
Roughly 3x the dollar cost (you pay every model) and 10x the latency of a single call (parallel answers plus a peer-review round plus chairman synthesis). What you gain is genuinely diverse views and a vote against any one model's failure modes. Worth it for high-stakes correctness, never worth it for cheap reads.
05Patterns It Uses

It decomposes into.

All three sub-patterns live in the Composition layer: every step combines or verifies outputs from multiple models.

L2 Combine: Ensembling N diverse models for parallel answers (round 1) L2 Verify: Anonymous peer review across council members (round 2) L2 Combine: Chairman synthesis of scored peer answers into one reply (round 3)
06When to Use vs Alternatives

The decision.

Use an LLM Council when correctness genuinely matters and you need cross-vendor robustness. For most other cases, cheaper patterns win.
