Stateless Retrieval

How the AI looks something up right when you ask, uses it to answer, then forgets it.

01The Idea

What is it?

Stateless retrieval searches your documents for the few passages that match the question and hands those to the AI, fresh, on every single ask.

02See It Work

Type a question. Watch it find the answer.

The system searches three pieces of text for the ones that match your question. Matching pieces light up and feed into the answer. Each search is independent: nothing carries over from previous runs.

03A Real Example

Where you've felt this.

A help-desk bot answers from your company's own documents
Tap to see why
Your handbook, policies, and product docs are split into small pieces and stored. When you ask a question, the system pulls the few pieces that match and feeds them to the model with your question. The model writes an answer grounded in your own text, not made-up trivia.
An AI search shows you exactly which page each line came from
Tap to see why
Because the system knows which pieces it pulled, it can show those sources next to the answer. You can tap any line and see the exact passage it came from. That is how an answer earns trust: you can check it.
An assistant answers about today's news without being retrained
Tap to see why
The model itself was trained months ago, but a fresh search runs at the moment you ask. It pulls today's articles, hands them to the model, and the answer comes back current. The model never had to relearn anything.
04Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

Setup work
Searching lets the model answer from your own content without retraining it. Each piece pulled in adds words to the prompt, which adds a little wait and a little cost. Good systems rank carefully so only the most useful pieces go in, then reuse recent searches to skip the rest.
