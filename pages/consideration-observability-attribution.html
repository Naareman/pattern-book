Tracing why it answered the way it did

Attribution links each part of the answer back to the input it came from: a specific document, a prior turn, a tool result, or the model's own pretraining.

01What It Is

For each sentence: where did it come from?

An answer is rarely one source speaking. It is a blend: pieces from the documents the system pulled in, hints from earlier turns of the conversation, results from a tool, and the model's own background knowledge. Attribution breaks the answer back into its pieces and shows which input shaped which claim. The simplest form is a citation next to each sentence. The richer form highlights the exact passages from the documents that the model leaned on. When a claim has no source, that absence is itself a signal: the model probably made it up.

02An Example

Tap a claim to see where it came from.

Answer to: "Tell me about Marie Curie's Nobel Prizes."

The answer

Marie Curie won two Nobel Prizes. The first was in Physics in 1903, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel. The second was in Chemistry in 1911 for her work on radium and polonium. She personally accepted both prizes in Stockholm.

What the model saw

doc 1Wikipedia, Marie Curie: "Curie was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, and the only person to win the prize in two scientific fields."
doc 2NobelPrize.org bio: "1903 Physics laureate (with Pierre Curie and A. H. Becquerel). 1911 Chemistry laureate, for the isolation of radium."
modelThe model's own pretraining knowledge. No retrieved source backs the highlighted claim.
The last claim has no source highlighted. That gap is a warning sign: the model added it on its own, and it may be wrong.
03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it is
A map from claim to source. Citations are the user-facing form. Token-level attribution is the deeper engineering form. Both answer the same question: where did this come from?
Sits next to
Pairs with tracing (the record of what was pulled in), retrieval quality (was the right document fetched?), and groundedness checks (does the claim actually appear in the source?).
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • The user needs to check the answer themselves: legal, medical, finance, anything stakes-heavy.
  • You suspect made-up content and want to see which claims have a source and which do not.
  • You want to tell apart a bad answer caused by bad retrieval from one caused by the model ignoring good sources.
