Tree-of-Thoughts

Have the model explore several candidate reasoning paths, evaluate them, and commit to the best one.

01What It Is

Explore paths, pick the best.

Tree-of-Thoughts treats reasoning as a search: the model branches into multiple candidate next-thoughts at each step, evaluates them, prunes dead ends, and backtracks, instead of following a single left-to-right chain.

02An Example

A 24-game puzzle, three paths explored.

Task: Make 24 from cards 4, 7, 8, 2
Start: {4, 7, 8, 2}
8 - 2 = 6
4 x 7 = 28 (fail)
4 x (8 - 2) = 24
Answer: 4 x 6 = 24 ✓
7 + 2 = 9
8 x ... (fail)
Two branches pruned early.  ·  One branch reaches 24.
03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it does
It is a search over many tries, not a single instruction in the prompt. Cost grows with how wide and deep the tree gets, which is the main thing to weigh.
Compare to
Chain-of-Thought writes one line of reasoning. Tree-of-Thoughts explores several and drops the weak ones as it goes. Self-Consistency runs many separate attempts and votes, but never prunes partway through.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • Problems that need real exploration: puzzles, proofs, planning.
  • Early wrong turns cannot be fixed by just reasoning forward.
  • You can afford the extra cost of many tries. For most tasks, simpler step-by-step reasoning is cheaper and enough.
