Provenance & watermarking

Attach a tamper-resistant tag to every image, video, or document the system makes. Anyone receiving it can check the tag and learn which model, which prompt, which moment.

01What is it?

What is it?

Provenance attaches a signed metadata card to every AI-made file (which model, which prompt, what time), and watermarking hides a signal inside the content itself so the origin is traceable even if the metadata is stripped.

02An example

Tap the image. Read its origin card.

An AI-made image is sent to a journalist. The badges sit alongside the file. The signed origin card is one tap away.

sunrise.png
AI-made Signed origin (C2PA) Pixel watermark
file: sunrise.png claim_generator: image-studio v4.2 model: flux-dev @ 2026-05-26 prompt: "sunrise over purple sky, soft, painterly" issuer: acme-design signature: valid (rsa-2048, c2pa) watermark: detected (synthid-image)
Provenance is the receipt. The watermark is the seal. Strip the receipt and the seal still tells the truth.
03Where it fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it covers: the bit of metadata or signal that travels with the output once it leaves your system, so the world can tell it apart from a human-made one.
Sits next to: the audit log records what your system did. Provenance records what the output is. The first is for you. The second is for whoever opens the file later.
Quick test: take an output your system made yesterday. Hand it to someone outside the team. Can they tell, with one tool, that it was made by your AI and by which model? If yes, provenance is real.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • You make images, video, or audio: deepfake risk and platform rules push you toward C2PA or similar signing.
  • You operate in the European Union: the AI Act requires generated and edited content to be machine-readable as AI-made.
  • You publish to journalists, regulators, or schools: people downstream need to tell what is original from what is generated.
