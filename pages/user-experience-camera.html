A camera that looks and answers

You point your phone at the world and the AI tells you what it sees. The question is the photo. The answer is whatever you wanted to know about it.

01What is it?

The world becomes the prompt.

A camera surface uses an image, or a live feed, as the way you ask. The model reads the picture and replies in words or actions. Google Lens identifies the plant. Be My Eyes describes the room to a blind user. ChatGPT's vision mode reads the screenshot you just took. The mental model is "show, don't type," which works in moments when you cannot describe a thing but you can point at it.

The photo replaces the typed question. No words to look up, no description to compose.

03Where it fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it is
A surface, not a skill. The camera is just the way the question goes in. The model behind it is a vision-capable language model.
Pairs with
Voice. People often want to point and ask out loud at the same time ("what is this plant?"), so camera and voice land well together on a phone.
Not the same as
An image search. Image search returns matching pictures. A camera surface returns an answer about the thing in front of you.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The user cannot easily describe the thing in words: an unknown plant, a foreign-language menu, an error message on a screen.
  • The answer depends on details a photo carries for free: brand, condition, layout, the exact line on a receipt.
  • Accessibility. For low-vision users, a camera plus a voice answer turns the visual world into something they can ask about.
