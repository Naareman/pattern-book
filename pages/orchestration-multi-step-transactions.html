Multi-Step Transactions

Treat a sequence of actions as one unit: either all of them happen, or none of them do. No half-finished orders, no orphan bookings.

01What is it?

All or nothing.

A multi-step transaction wraps several actions so they all succeed or the wrapper automatically unwinds whatever already ran.

02An Example

A booking that finishes or vanishes.

All four steps succeed. The transaction commits as one unit.
1Reserve seatok
2Hold hotelok
3Charge cardok
4Send confirmationok
Result: trip booked, card charged, confirmation in inbox.
The card is declined at step 3. The transaction unwinds the earlier steps using their compensating actions.
1Reserve seatundone
2Hold hotelreleased
3Charge cardfailed
4Send confirmationskipped
Result: the user sees a clean "could not book" message. No phantom hold, no orphan reservation.
03Where It Fits

Where it sits.

Builds on
External action, idempotency, and compensating actions. The transaction is the wrapper that uses all three to give an all-or-nothing guarantee.
Not the same as
A single database transaction. That works only inside one system. A multi-step transaction spans several services, so it has to undo with real-world actions.
Often called
A saga, in distributed systems writing. The agent kicks it off, the saga coordinator does the work.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • A user-visible outcome depends on several actions, and a partial result would leave them confused or out of pocket.
  • The actions touch different systems (payments, inventory, messaging) that have no shared rollback of their own.
  • You need a written record of what happened, in order, so audits and incident reviews are easy.
