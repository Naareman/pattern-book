Cost

Every word the AI reads or writes costs real money. Keep it cheap enough and everyone can use it. Let it run unchecked and the bill ends the product.

01The Idea

What costs money?

AI models charge per token: roughly one word, in or out. Every character you send and every character the model writes adds to the bill. A longer prompt, a bigger model, or a thousand users asking the same question all drive the cost up.

Three levers bring it back down: write shorter prompts (fewer tokens in), pick a smaller model for easy tasks (cheaper per token), and cache repeated answers (pay once, serve many).

03A Real Example

Two products, same feature.

Tap a scenario to see how cost discipline (or lack of it) plays out in practice.

Startup A: big model, every request Costly
Routes every query, including "what's the capital of France?", through the largest frontier model. At 10 000 daily users the bill hits $12 000/month. Revenue: $4 000/month. Forced to shut the product to new sign-ups within 60 days.
Startup B: route by complexity + cache Sustainable
Simple questions go to a small cheap model. Complex ones go to the large model. Answers to the 200 most common questions are cached. Same 10 000 daily users, same features. Bill: $1 400/month. The product stays open and grows.
Enterprise: shorter system prompts Quick win
The system prompt (instructions sent with every query) was 2 400 tokens of verbose policy text. After trimming it to 600 tokens without changing behavior, input costs dropped by 75% across millions of monthly calls. No model change, no caching, just fewer words.

04Tradeoffs

What you give up.

lowhigh
Shorter prompts
Smaller model
Caching
Shorter prompts risk losing important context. Smaller models may miss nuance on hard questions. Caching only helps when queries repeat, and cached answers can go stale. None of these is free. The skill is knowing when the savings outweigh the loss.
