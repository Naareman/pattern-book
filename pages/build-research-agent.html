Research Agent

Looks things up across many sources, then writes up what it found.

01The Idea

What is it?

A Research Agent splits your question into smaller ones, searches several sources at once, and writes a short summary with citations.

Orchestration & agents Stateless Retrieval
02See It Work

Watch it fan out.

The agent receives one question, splits it into searches, fans them out, then synthesises the findings.

Question
What are the health effects of drinking coffee daily?
1 Plan: break into sub-questions waiting
Sub-questions to search:
  1. Short-term effects of caffeine on alertness
  2. Long-term cardiovascular effects of daily coffee
  3. Coffee and cancer risk (research summary)
2 Search: fan out across sources waiting
PubMed Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, improving alertness for 3-5 hours. ✓
Harvard Health 3-5 cups/day linked to lower risk of heart failure and stroke in large cohort studies. ✓
WHO Review Coffee not classifiable as carcinogenic; some evidence of lower colorectal cancer risk. ✓
3 Synthesise: write the summary waiting
Daily coffee (3-5 cups) is well-tolerated for most adults. Short-term: caffeine sharpens alertness by blocking adenosine. Long-term: large studies link moderate intake to lower cardiovascular risk. On cancer: current evidence does not support a harmful link, and some studies suggest modest protective effects for colorectal cancer.
Sources: PubMed, Harvard Health, WHO International Agency for Research on Cancer (2016)

05Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

lowhigh

Accuracy
More sources means more cost and more waiting. But for questions that span many topics, the accuracy jump is worth it. For simple, single-fact questions, a single call is faster and cheaper.
