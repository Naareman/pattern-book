Self-Ask

The model explicitly asks itself the sub-questions it needs to answer before tackling the original question, turning a hard leap into a chain of smaller steps.

01What It Is

Questions before the answer.

Rather than jumping straight to a final answer, the model is prompted to first write out the follow-up questions it would need to resolve in order to answer reliably. It answers each sub-question in sequence, building up intermediate facts. Only then does it compose the final answer from those verified pieces. The technique is named for the explicit follow-up question format the model uses: "Follow-up: ... Intermediate answer: ..." repeated until the chain bottoms out and the final answer can be stated with confidence.

02An Example

Answering a multi-hop geography question.

Self-Ask trace
Who was the president of the country that first landed humans on the moon, when that landing happened?
Q1
Which country first landed humans on the moon?
The United States (Apollo 11, July 1969).
Q2
Who was the president of the United States in July 1969?
Richard Nixon (took office January 1969).
Final answer: Richard Nixon was president of the United States when Apollo 11 landed on the moon in July 1969.
03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it does
All the sub-questions and their answers happen inside one model call. The model checks its own understanding by resolving each piece before it commits to a final answer.
Compare to
Least-to-Most prompting also breaks a hard question into smaller ones, but it focuses on building up from easy to hard. Self-Ask's signature move is the explicit follow-up question used as a self-check.
Not this
If each sub-question kicks off a real web search or a fresh model call, that is a multi-step agent or retrieval setup, not Self-Ask. Self-Ask keeps every step inside the one answer.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • Questions that need several facts chained together, where one big leap would skip a step.
  • Knowledge-heavy tasks like history, geography, law, and science.
  • When you need the reasoning to be auditable: the follow-up questions show exactly where it went wrong.
