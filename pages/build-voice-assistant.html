Voice Assistant

You talk out loud. It talks back. Speed is what makes it feel real.

01The Idea

What is it?

A voice assistant turns your speech into text, writes a reply, and speaks it back, fast enough that it feels like a real conversation.

Single-call control Serving & infrastructure
02See it Work

The pipeline, live.

Tap Play to run a request through all three stages. Watch what passes between them, and see how total latency builds up.

Step 1
Speech to Text
Your microphone captures audio. A fast model transcribes it to plain text in real time.
→ "What's the weather in Cairo?"
Step 2
AI Reply
The text question goes to the language model. It generates a short spoken reply, streamed out token by token.
→ "It's 32°C and sunny in Cairo today."
Step 3
Text to Speech
The reply text is synthesized into audio and streamed to your speaker, often starting before the full sentence is ready.
→ spoken audio out

04When it Matters

Latency is the product.

Same assistant, same answer. Only the total latency changes. Here is what each feels like to a real user:

Under 800 ms
natural
Feels live
800 ms to 2 s
noticeable
Feels slow
Over 2 s
waiting…
Feels broken

This is why voice assistant infrastructure runs on dedicated, low-latency servers and why the AI model must stream its reply immediately, not wait for the full answer before handing it to the text-to-speech stage. Every millisecond is felt.

05Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

lowhigh
Wait time
Infrastructure cost
Error surface
Reply quality
Wait time is the hardest constraint. Each of the three stages can fail independently (bad audio, hallucinated reply, robotic speech), so the error surface is wider than a text-only product. Reply quality is intentionally lower: spoken answers must be short or they take too long to say.
