Latency budgets across modalities

Images and audio take longer to upload, longer to process, and turn into more tokens than the same idea written in text. Plan the wait, piece by piece.

01What Is It?

Time, broken into pieces.

Wait time for a multimodal call is the sum of several steps: the user's input travels to the server (small for text, big for an image, bigger for a video), the server turns it into tokens or features (vision encoders take real time, audio transcription takes seconds), the model thinks (slower because images become hundreds of tokens), and the answer travels back. A latency budget assigns a time cap to each step and a total cap for the user. When one step blows its slice, that is the one to fix.

02An Example

Photo question, broken down.

Upload image
~0.5 s
Encode image
~0.8 s
Model thinks
~1.5 s
Stream answer
~0.6 s
Total budget: ~3.4 s. The same question as plain text would be roughly 1 second. The image adds upload, encoding, and more thinking tokens. Knowing where the time goes tells you what to optimise: resize the image, switch to a faster vision encoder, or stream the answer sooner.

04When It Matters

When to think about it.

  • The product feels snappy on text and sluggish the moment an image or audio clip is sent.
  • You have a real-time target (voice assistant, live camera assistant) and need every step to fit inside it.
  • Tail latency is showing up: most calls are fast, a small number drag the average up.
