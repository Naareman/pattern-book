Eval methodology

The recipe for grading the system: who picks the test cases, who writes the rubric, who gives the score, and how the score is read.

01What It Is

A grading plan, written down.

An eval methodology is the documented recipe for judging quality: a fixed set of test cases, a written rubric, a chosen grader, and a clear way to read the score so two people reach the same verdict.

02An Example

Four pieces of a support-bot eval.

01 Cases Golden set 120 real customer questions, sampled across topics, frozen and version-tagged.
02 Rubric Scoring rules Each answer is rated on accuracy, tone, and whether it cites the right policy page.
03 Grader Who scores Two support leads score every answer; a model double-checks for a quick sanity pass.
04 Readout How to read it Report pass-rate per topic with a confidence interval, alongside last month's number.
Each piece is decided once and reused for every run, so today's score compares cleanly to yesterday's.
03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it is
The plan for how to grade, not the grader itself. It applies to any AI system, big or small.
Sits next to
Pairs with the golden test set kept as a versioned artifact, and with whatever rule decides what a good answer is for your specific use case.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • You ship a real product: a stable score on a fixed test set is the cheapest way to know each change made things better.
  • Different people argue about whether a recent change helped: a written rubric ends the argument with a number.
  • You need to show a stakeholder how quality moves week over week.
