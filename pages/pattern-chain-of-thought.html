Pattern

Chain-of-Thought

01The Idea

What is Chain-of-Thought?

Chain-of-Thought is one line you add to your prompt, like "Let's think step by step." The model then shows its working before answering, and that working is what makes the answer right.
02Data Flow

How it works.

Watch the model answer one small question. Instead of jumping to a number, it writes its thinking out loud, line by line, and the answer falls out of the last line.

Question 3 baskets, each with 6 apples. I eat 4 apples. How many are left?
Press Play to watch the model write its thinking.
1 There are 3 baskets, and each one has 6 apples.
2 So in total: 3 × 6 = 18 apples.
3 I eat 4 of those 18, so 18 − 4 = 14.
Answer 14 apples are left.
 
An apple problem with two small steps. A guess could miss it. Watch what the model does instead.
A token is a small piece of text, about a word:
Let 's think step by step .
The model writes one at a time, left to right.

04Worked Example

One real case.

User asks
"A crew of 4 painters each finish 3 rooms a day. They work 5 days. The building has 70 rooms. How many are left unpainted?"
1
Pull out the numbers
4 painters, 3 rooms each per day, 5 days, 70 rooms total. Listing the facts before doing any math is what reasoning first means.
2
Step one, per day
How many rooms get painted in a single day? 4 painters × 3 rooms = 12 rooms / day
3
Step two, over five days
Use the answer from the step before, don't start over. 12 rooms/day × 5 days = 60 rooms painted
4
Step three, what's left
Subtract painted from the total. 70 rooms − 60 painted = 10 left
Answer returned to user
"10 rooms are left unpainted." The answer is just the last step, and every step before it was easy to check. Without the steps, models often jump to a wrong number here.
05Code Sketch

The shape in code.

Tap a line to see what it does. The whole pattern is one change to the prompt.
# the trick: ask for the reasoning before the answer
prompt = question + "\nLet's think step by step."
# the model writes its steps, then lands on an answer
response = model(prompt)
# reasoning is everything above; the answer is the last line
answer = response.splitlines()[-1]
return answer, response

06Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

Cost
Wait time
Complexity
Asking the model to think step by step helps a lot on hard problems. On a simple question it just adds extra words, so you pay more (tokens) for no better answer.
