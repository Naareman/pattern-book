Stateful Memory

How an AI remembers what was said and what you prefer, across this chat and across future ones.

01The Idea

What is it?

An AI has no built-in memory between conversations. Each request starts fresh. Stateful memory is what adds persistence: the system stores what was said, what you prefer, or what happened in a past session, then writes that context back into the message you send the AI when it is useful. Every remembered piece costs a token (a small piece the AI reads, roughly a word), so good memory systems are careful about what they keep, what they shrink, and what they drop.

03A Real Example

Where you've felt this.

The chatbot answers your "and what about it?" without missing a beat

Every time you send a message, the whole back-and-forth so far is sent with it. The word "it" makes sense because the earlier exchange is right there in front of the AI as it reads your new line.
It remembers your name and your style from weeks ago
Tap to see why
A separate store keeps facts you have shared (your name, what you like, your tone). When a new chat starts, the system quietly looks up what is on file for you and slips the relevant bits into the message before the AI reads it.
You can ask "what did we decide about the logo last week?" and it knows
Tap to see why
Past sessions are saved as small, searchable notes. When you mention something from before, the system finds the matching note, pulls the relevant lines, and hands them to the AI alongside your current question.
You delete a fact, and the AI stops bringing it up
Tap to see why
Memory has a delete button on purpose. When you remove an item, the system drops it from the store, so it is never pulled in again. Good memory systems also let stale or low-value facts quietly fade on their own.
04Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

lowhigh
Feels personal
Message gets longer
Cost per answer
Private info to manage
Stale or wrong recall
Memory makes the AI feel personal and continuous. Every remembered turn and stored fact also lengthens the message, raising cost and slowing the reply. Longer memory builds up private information that has to be cared for. Good systems shrink old memories, drop what is no longer useful, and let you delete what you want.
