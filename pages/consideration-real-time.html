Real-time

Answering fast enough that it feels instant. The gap before the first word matters far more than the total time.

01The Idea

What does real-time mean?

When you ask an AI something, it has to generate every word before sending it. The wait from "I sent the question" to "I saw the first word" is called time-to-first-token. It is the number that determines whether an AI feels instant or sluggish. One trick changes everything: streaming, which sends each word as it is generated instead of waiting for the full answer. The total time is the same. The felt wait drops to almost nothing.

03When it Matters

Where every millisecond counts.

Voice assistants
People expect a spoken reply to start within about 500ms. A two-second pause breaks the illusion of conversation entirely. Streaming audio lets the AI speak the first sentence while still generating the rest.
time-to-first-token critical
AI code editors (autocomplete)
Suggestions that arrive in under 100ms feel like the editor is reading your mind. Suggestions that arrive after a second feel like an interruption. Speed here is the product.
sub-100ms target
Fraud detection
A payment must be approved or blocked while the customer is still at the register. A correct decision that arrives five seconds late is a failed product. Latency here is measured in tens of milliseconds.
hard latency deadline
04Tradeoffs

What you give up for speed.

lowhigh
Infrastructure cost
Complexity added
Model quality impact
Faster hardware and more replicas cost real money. Streaming requires clients to handle partial responses. But the quality of the answer is not affected by streaming: the model generates the same tokens either way. Speed and correctness are separate knobs.
