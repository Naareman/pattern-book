Consideration

Real-time

Answering fast enough that it feels instant, like talking to a person.

In plain words

People do not like waiting. Real-time means the AI replies quickly, often starting to answer within a second. The time you wait for a reply is called latency, and keeping it low is what makes an AI feel snappy instead of sluggish.

Why it matters

What goes wrong if you ignore it

A voice assistant takes eight seconds to answer a simple question. By the time it speaks, the user has already repeated themselves or given up. For things like live translation or self-driving, a slow answer is not just annoying, it can be useless or unsafe.

Where it shows up most

Rough editorial estimate of where this matters most, not a measured score. Speed is won or lost mostly in serving and infrastructure, where the model actually runs, with a strong knock-on effect on how the experience feels.

Examples

Streaming replies: chatbots show words as they are generated so you start reading right away.
Live translation in video calls converts speech almost as fast as people talk.
Fraud detection must approve or block a card payment in the moment it happens.
