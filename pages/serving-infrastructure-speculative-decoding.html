Drafting fast, then checking

A tiny fast model writes a quick draft of the next few words. The big model checks the whole draft in one shot and keeps the words it agrees with.

01What is it?

Two models, same answer, fewer waits.

Speculative decoding pairs a small fast drafter with the real big model. The drafter guesses several next words; the big model reviews them in one pass and accepts the ones it would have said anyway. The reply still matches what the big model alone would have produced, with fewer round trips to write it.

02An Example

Draft a few words, verify in one shot.

Step 1 · Small model drafts cheap and quick
Thecatsatontheredmat
Step 2 · Big model verifies all seven words at once
Thecatsatontheredsoft

The big model agrees with the first five words, swaps the sixth, and the draft stops there. The next round starts after "soft".

Net effect: five accepted words in one big-model pass instead of five separate passes. The output is exactly what the big model would have written on its own.
03Where it fits

Where it sits.

Common uses
Inside the serving engine, turned on as an option. Latency-focused providers use it to make chat feel quicker without changing the model behind it.
Sits next to
Reusing work within an answer. Spec decoding speeds up how many words come out per pass; cache reuse keeps each pass cheap.
Different from
Picking a smaller, cheaper model. That changes the answer. Speculative decoding keeps the big model's answer, just gets there faster.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • You care about how quickly each word appears on the user's screen.
  • You can afford the extra small model running alongside the big one.
  • Your traffic is mostly predictable text (chat, code, summaries) where a small drafter guesses correctly most of the time.
