Citation & provenance

Attach a source tag to each fact in the answer, then let the user tap the tag to see the exact passage. That is how an answer earns trust.

01What is it?

A tap-to-check tag on every claim.

Citations label each line in the answer with the source passage that supported it, so the reader can tap any tag and verify the claim directly.

02An example

Tap a number, see the source.

Answer with source tags · tap any tag

Full-time staff receive 22 paid leave days per year . Leave is requested through the manager portal, and any block longer than two days needs seven days of notice . The office is closed from December 24 to January 2; those days do not count against personal leave .

Source 1 · Handbook page 12 "Full-time staff receive 22 paid leave days per year, granted in half-day units, refreshed on each work anniversary."

Each tag points at the exact passage the model used for that line. If a tag points at the wrong place, you have caught a mistake.

03Where it fits

Where it fits.

Runs after
The model answers. The system already kept the list of pieces it sent in, so it can map facts in the answer back to those pieces.
Builds trust
A citation gives the user one tap to verify a claim. It changes the answer from "trust me" to "see for yourself".
Watch for
Fake citations: the model sometimes invents a tag that points at a piece that does not back the claim. Always check that the cited passage really says what the answer says.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The answer is used to make a decision: legal, medical, financial, or policy.
  • Users keep asking "where did you get that?" and have no way to check.
  • You need a paper trail for audits or compliance.
