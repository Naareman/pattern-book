Caching

Many users ask the same thing within minutes. Save the search result the first time, then serve the saved copy for repeats so the work happens only once.

01What is it?

Store a result so the next person gets it instantly.

Caching saves the search result the first time a question arrives, then serves the saved copy for repeats so the retrieval work happens only once.

02An example

One hour, five questions, two real searches.

Five users, one help-desk bot 3 of 5 served from cache
09:01 How many vacation days do I get? Miss 820 ms
09:04 How many vacation days do I get?(repeat) Hit 35 ms
09:09 How do I reset my password? Miss 780 ms
09:11 How many vacation days do I get?(repeat) Hit 28 ms
09:18 How do I reset my password?(repeat) Hit 31 ms
What changed: only two of the five questions hit the real search. The other three were served from a stored copy in milliseconds. Same answers, a fraction of the work.
03Where it fits

Where it fits.

Sits in front of
Every search and live look-up. The cache is checked first; the heavier work only runs on a miss.
Pairs with
A way to notice when content is old. A cache alone is happy to serve a stale answer; pairing it with a freshness rule keeps that from happening.
Watch for
Caching errors. If a search fails, store the failure and you have just made it permanent. Only cache real answers.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • A small group of questions makes up most of your traffic.
  • Search costs or wait times are showing up on your bill or in user feedback.
  • The same answer is good for at least a few minutes before the underlying content changes.
