Chain-of-Verification

The model generates an answer, lists the factual claims inside it, turns each claim into a verification question, then answers those questions to catch its own errors.

01What It Is

Facts interrogated, not assumed.

After drafting a response, the model breaks it into individual factual claims, answers a verification question for each one independently, and revises before returning the final answer.

02An Example

Checking a draft about Marie Curie.

Claim in the draft
Verification question + result
Marie Curie won two Nobel Prizes.
How many Nobel Prizes did Marie Curie win?
Answer: two (Physics 1903, Chemistry 1911).
Confirmed
She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize in any field.
Was she the first woman Nobel laureate in any field?
Answer: yes, for Physics in 1903.
Confirmed
She was born in Warsaw in 1868.
When was Marie Curie born?
Answer: 7 November 1867, not 1868.
Mismatch: revise
The mismatch on the birth year is caught inside the same call. The model corrects the draft before returning a final answer.
03Where It Fits

Where it fits.

Home
A way of getting the model to check its own work. Everything happens inside one call to one model: the draft, pulling out the claims, the questions, and the fix.
Not this
If a second call (or a separate checker model) reviews the claims, that is a different layer. The line is the number of calls: one call stays here, two calls move on.
In short: the model checks itself inside one call. Bring in a separate checker call or a different model and you have moved to a different layer.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • Facts must be exact, and a second model call would be too slow or too costly.
  • Best on fact-heavy work (biography, science, legal citations) where a confident draft can still slip a detail.
  • It will not fill gaps the model never knew. For that, add a step that looks information up first.
