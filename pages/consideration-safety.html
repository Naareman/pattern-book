Safety, Alignment & Refusal

The discipline of keeping AI behavior within intended bounds: deciding what to refuse, how severely to score harm, and how to balance safety against helpfulness. The mechanisms that emit a refusal live in Single-Call Control (output shaping) and User Surface (the refusal message); this crosscut is the framework for reasoning about all of it.

01The Idea

What is it?

Every request to an AI passes through a filter called a guardrail. It checks: is this harmful? If yes, refuse and stop. The model never even sees the bad request. A second guardrail does the same on the way out, catching anything the model should not have written. The goal is simple: dangerous requests get blocked, safe ones go through.

03What This Covers

The disciplines inside this crosscut.

Applies to: every AI system.
Axis: keeping AI behavior within intended bounds.
Note on scope. This crosscut is a discipline: frameworks for reasoning about what to allow, what to refuse, and how to calibrate severity. The mechanisms that actually emit a refusal live in Single-Call Control (L4) (output shaping) and User Surface (L7) (the refusal message the user sees). Both are cross-referenced here.

Setting the bounds

Refusal Calibration
Concerns where the line sits between refusing and helping, and how often it lands in the wrong place.
Harm Taxonomies & Severity Reasoning
Concerns how harmful content is organized into named categories and scored by seriousness before a response decision is made.
Refusal-vs-Helpfulness Tradeoff
Concerns how often safety measures block legitimate requests, and how to tune that balance without tightening or loosening everything at once.
Principle-Based Behavioral Evaluation
Concerns whether the model's behavior matches a stated set of values across many scenarios, not just specific known-bad inputs.

Testing the bounds

Red-Teaming Methodology
Concerns how to proactively attack your own system with adversarial prompts before bad actors do, then use what you find to close gaps.
Jailbreak-Resistance Frameworks
Concerns how the system is designed so that clever rephrasing, role-play framings, or multi-step tricks cannot bypass the safety logic.
Dual-Use Review Processes
Concerns content that is genuinely useful to most people but could enable harm for a few, and how to handle it consistently.

05Tradeoffs

The tension you cannot avoid.

lowhigh
False refusals
Added latency
Maintenance cost
Tighter guardrails catch more harm but also block more legitimate requests. Looser ones are more useful but let more through. There is no perfect setting: every deployment picks a point on this curve based on its risk tolerance. Guardrail rules also drift as language and misuse patterns change, so they need ongoing review.
