User Experience

Everything the person actually sees, taps, and steers: where you meet the AI, how the answer shows up, and how you stay in control of what it does.

01The Idea

What is it?

The user experience layer is everything between the AI's raw output and the person reading it: the words streaming in, the sources shown, the buttons to copy, retry, or say if it helped.

03A Real Example

What each piece actually does.

Streaming
ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini all stream: they send the answer word by word the moment each one is ready, instead of waiting until the full reply is done. This makes a 10-second reply feel like a conversation, not a page load.
Sources
Perplexity shows small numbered source pills next to each sentence. Tap one and it opens the exact page. This lets you check the answer and builds trust, because you can see where it came from.
Copy / Try again
Copy puts the answer on the clipboard in one tap. Try again sends the same question once more to get a different attempt, useful when the first answer missed the point.
Thumbs Up / Down
Every AI product collects this signal. Teams use it to find answers people disliked, where it feeds the work that shapes future answers. One tap turns into a data point teams can act on.
04Tradeoffs

The price you pay.

lowhigh
Perceived speed
User control
Build complexity
Feedback signal
Streaming the answer word by word raises the feeling of speed a lot, but the connection has to stay open the whole time. Thumbs feedback is sparse and noisy: most people never press it, and a thumbs-down could mean wrong answer, wrong tone, or just frustration. Showing sources looks simple but needs a system underneath that tracks where every claim came from, which adds work to build.
