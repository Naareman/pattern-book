Rerank the best matches

Search pulls back a wide pile of plausible pieces. A second pass reads each one against the question and reorders them so the strongest matches sit at the top.

01What is it?

A second look at the search results.

The first search step is fast and casts a wide net: it grabs maybe twenty plausible pieces. A reranker is a smaller, slower model that scores each of those twenty against the actual question, one pair at a time. The few that score highest go to the model; the rest are dropped. The same model that ran the search would have been too slow to do this for every piece in the whole library, which is why it happens in two stages.

02An example

Same six pieces, better order.

Question: how do I reset my password?
After first search
1
Password rules: 12 characters, one symbol.
2
Forgot username? See the IT portal.
3
Reset link: open settings, then "Reset password".
4
Two-factor login setup steps.
5
If reset email never arrives, contact the help desk.
6
Account lockout after 5 failed attempts.
→
After rerank (top 3 kept)
1
Reset link: open settings, then "Reset password".
2
If reset email never arrives, contact the help desk.
3
Password rules: 12 characters, one symbol.
What changed: the rerank reads each piece against the actual question, not just shared words. The "reset" steps move to the top; the lockout policy and two-factor setup drop off.
03Where it fits

Where it fits.

Runs after
Search. Rerank only ever sees the short list the search step handed it. It cannot pull in pieces the search missed.
Hands off to
The step that packs the prompt. Only the top few pieces continue forward, so the prompt stays small.
The tradeoff
Reranking adds a small wait and a small cost per question. In return, the model sees a much cleaner shortlist and answers more reliably.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The model often answers from the wrong piece, even when the right piece is among the top twenty.
  • You want to keep the prompt small while still casting a wide search net.
  • Questions are subtle: the search step can match plausible words but miss the actual point.
