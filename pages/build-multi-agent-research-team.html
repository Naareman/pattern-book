Multi-Agent Research Team

A lead agent (the AI model, or LLM) splits a question into parts, hands each part to a worker agent, and merges their findings into one report.

01The Idea

What is it?

A Multi-Agent Research Team is a lead AI that splits your question into sub-topics, hands each to a worker AI to research in parallel, then stitches their findings into one report.

02See It Work

Watch the team fan out.

The lead agent receives one question, assigns a sub-topic to each of three workers, all three research in parallel, then their findings are merged into one answer.

Question (the prompt sent to the lead agent)
How is AI changing the job market for knowledge workers?

The lead agent breaks the question into three sub-topics and assigns one to each worker:
  1. Worker A: which jobs are most affected and why?
  2. Worker B: which new roles are AI creating?
  3. Worker C: what do the economic studies say about net job impact?

AI is reshaping knowledge work along three fronts. First, roles heavy in routine text tasks (legal review, data entry, first-draft writing) face significant task substitution, with some studies estimating 30-60% of tasks automatable. Second, new specialist roles are emerging fast: prompt engineers, AI trainers, and model auditors are among the highest-demand titles. Third, the net job impact is positive in aggregate but uneven in timing: the IMF estimates 60% of advanced-economy jobs are affected, with productivity gains likely outpacing displacement over the medium term.
Sources: Worker A (McKinsey 2024), Worker B (LinkedIn Emerging Jobs 2024), Worker C (IMF World Economic Outlook 2024)

04Patterns It Uses

Which layers do the work.

Orchestration & Agents: lead plans and fans work out to workers in parallel Orchestration & Agents: shared workspace lets the lead read all findings at merge time Stateless Retrieval: each worker's searches are read-only, nothing changes in the world Composition: splits the question into sub-topics, recombines findings into one report
