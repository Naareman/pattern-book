Ensembling

Ask the same question a few times and merge the answers into one. Voting catches one-off mistakes; averaging or blending smooths out noise.

01What is it?

Many tries, one answer.

Run the prompt several times (with different settings, prompts, or models), then combine those answers by voting on the most common one, averaging the scores, or blending the text into a single output.

02An Example

Three tries, vote wins.

Question
If a train leaves at 2:15 and travels for 1h 50min, what time does it arrive?
↓
Final answer (majority vote)
4:05
Tap a card to see each attempt.
Two out of three tries agree. The odd one out gets dropped. The final answer is the one most tries landed on.
03Where it fits

How it sits with nearby ideas.

Pairs with
Often used with chain-of-thought: get the model to reason a few different ways, then vote on which final answer shows up most.
Compare to
Picking the best uses one judge to choose a winner. Ensembling skips the judge: it just counts which answer comes up most often.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The question has one right answer (math, classification, structured fields) so votes are easy to count.
  • Answers vary call to call and you want a steadier result.
  • You can afford to run the prompt a few times in parallel.
