Time-to-first-token (TTFT)

The wait between asking and seeing the first word appear. It is the single number users feel most.

01What is it?

The wait that users feel.

When the user hits send, two clocks start. One is the total time until the answer is fully written. The other is the time until the first word lands on screen. That second clock is time to first word, often called time to first token in engineering. It is the gap between the request leaving and the first piece of text arriving. Once words begin to appear, attention switches from waiting to reading, and the rest of the answer can take longer without feeling slow. So the first word is the latency budget that matters most for a live experience.

02An example

Same total time, very different feel.

Two ways to spend the same 3 seconds
Version A
2.7s wait
Version B
0.4s wait
Both take 3 seconds end to end. Version A feels broken because nothing happens for almost the whole time. Version B feels lively because the answer starts at the half-second mark and keeps going.
03Where it fits

How it sits next to nearby ideas.

What it is
A way of measuring lateness. It picks one moment (the first word) and treats that as the user-facing deadline.
Sits next to
It pairs with total latency (how long the whole answer takes) and throughput (how many tokens per second arrive). The three numbers together describe the shape of a streamed reply.
Not this
It is a target to hit, not a technique. Streaming as a strategy is the move that gets you there.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • Any product where a person is watching the screen waiting for an answer.
  • Voice and chat, where silence past a second or two reads as broken.
  • Picking between models or routing strategies, where the first-word number often matters more than the total time.
