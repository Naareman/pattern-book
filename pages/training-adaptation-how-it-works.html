Training & Adaptation

How the AI is taught and shaped, step by step, before you ever talk to it.

01The Idea

What is it?

Before you ever send a message, the AI goes through rounds of school: it reads a huge pile of text to learn language, studies worked examples for a specific job, then learns from human ratings of its own answers.

03A Real Example

How a chatbot like ChatGPT was made.

Round 1: reading at scale
The model was trained on trillions of words: books, articles, code, websites.
This gave it general knowledge and the ability to produce fluent text in many languages and styles.
Round 2: worked examples
Staff wrote thousands of ideal question-and-answer pairs and trained the model to copy that style.
This is what turned a next-word predictor into a conversational assistant that follows instructions.
Round 3: human ratings
Human raters compared pairs of answers and picked the better one. The model learned to predict those picks, then aimed for them.
This is what made it warmer, more helpful, and less likely to say harmful things.
04Tradeoffs

Freshness of knowledge
Training is the most powerful lever but the slowest and most expensive one. Knowledge has a cutoff date. Fine-tuning is costly to repeat. Human ratings can teach overconfidence if raters disagree. How you prompt the trained model still matters just as much as how it was trained.
05What each stage is for

Pick by goal.

Build the base

Want to teach it language from scratch? → Reading at scale
Want to keep general ability but shift it into a new field? → Topping up on new material
Want to track whether the model is improving as it trains? → Checking progress along the way

Tune to behave

Want to tune it for a job using worked examples? → Copying ideal answers
Want to shape its manner using human or AI ratings of which answer is better? → Learning from preference ratings
Want to build native step-by-step thinking into the model itself? → Training it to reason

Make smaller

Want to fine-tune cheaply, without retraining the whole model? → Adjusting only a tiny piece
Want to squeeze a big model's skill into a small one? → Teaching a small model from a big one
Want to blend two trained models into one without extra training? → Blending trained models

Sources
  • Brown et al. (2020). Language Models are Few-Shot Learners. The GPT-3 paper. Foundational large-scale pretraining result.
  • Ouyang et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. The InstructGPT paper that introduced learning from human ratings (RLHF) for chatbots.
  • OpenAI (2023). GPT-4 Technical Report. Describes the full pretraining and human-feedback pipeline for GPT-4.
  • Hu et al. (2022). LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models. Foundational work on adjusting only a tiny piece of a model for cheap fine-tuning.
  • Bai et al. (2022). Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback. A scalable alternative that uses AI rather than human ratings.
  • Rafailov et al. (2023). Direct Preference Optimization. A simpler way to learn from preference ratings, without a separate reward model.
