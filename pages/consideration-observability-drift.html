Spotting when behavior shifts over time

Drift detection watches a few simple measures (refusal rate, average length, tool-call rate, quality score) day after day and alerts when one steps outside its usual range.

01What It Is

A behavior the system used to have.

Even when nothing in your code changed, the answers can. A new model version goes live. The questions users ask shift. A retrieval index gets reindexed. Drift is the gap between what the system used to do and what it does now. The basic move is to pick a small set of measures, lock in a baseline from a healthy period, then watch the same measures each day. When a measure drifts past a threshold, the system raises a flag. The flag is a signal to look, not a diagnosis: drift tells you something moved, then you go find out what.

Drift alert Refusal rate climbed from a baseline of 4% to 18% on day 7. The bot started turning down questions it used to answer. Time to look at what changed: a new safety filter, a new model version, or a flood of unusual questions.
Each measure is a thin signal on its own. Together they show whether the system is behaving the way it used to.
03Where It Fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it is
A standing watch on a few measures. It will not tell you why something changed. It will tell you when something changed, which is the hard part.
Sits next to
Pairs with tracing (so you can open the requests behind a spike), evaluation (so you have a quality score to track), and incident response (the playbook for when a flag becomes a problem).
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • You depend on a hosted model whose version can change under you without warning.
  • The user mix or the topics change with the season (sales periods, exam weeks, news cycles).
  • You ship prompt or retrieval changes often and need a fast read on whether each release moved real behavior.
