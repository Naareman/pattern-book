Stateless Retrieval · Find

Indexing & embedding

Before the system can search well, the documents have to be cut into small, labeled pieces and stored in a way that makes them easy to look up later.

01What is it?

Cut the big pile into small pieces the system can search.

Document preparation splits each source into short, labeled passages so the search step can point at the exact piece that matches a question instead of loading the whole document.

02An example

One handbook, twelve pieces.

Employee handbook
One 84-page PDF. Long sections on benefits, leave policy, expenses, code of conduct, security, and onboarding all sit together. The search step has nothing small to match against, so it would have to load the whole thing.
Piece 1 · BenefitsHealth, dental, and vision plans start on day one.
Piece 2 · LeavePaid leave is 22 days a year, taken in half-day units.
Piece 3 · ExpensesReceipts above 50 dollars need a manager sign-off.
Piece 4 · ConductReport concerns through the ethics line within 7 days.
Piece 5 · SecuritySet up two-factor login before your first laptop sync.
Piece 6 · OnboardingBuddy assignments happen on day 3, after orientation.
What changed: the same handbook is now twelve tagged pieces. A search for "how many leave days" can land on piece 2 alone, instead of returning the whole book.
03Where it fits

Where it fits.

Sits before
Search. Cutting and storing comes first; the search step needs small pieces to match against.
Done once
This step runs ahead of time when documents arrive or change. The user never waits for it during a question.
Sets the ceiling
If the pieces are too big, search drags in clutter. If they are too small, the meaning gets cut in half. The shape of the pieces decides how good the answers can be.
04When it matters

When to focus on it.

  • Your documents are long: PDFs, manuals, policy books, transcripts.
  • Answers feel vague even though the right text is in your files.
  • Search is fast but the model still gets the wrong section.
