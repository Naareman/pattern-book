Topping it up on new material

Take a model that already knows English and feed it a focused new pile (medical papers, legal cases, a fresh year of news) so the same skill carries into that world.

01What is it?

More reading on a narrower shelf.

The same "guess the next word" exercise from the first round, but the material is swapped out for a slice of text the team cares about, so the model picks up the vocabulary, conventions, and recent facts of that slice without forgetting what it already knew.

02An example

A medical model needs much less reading than the original.

Tiny in comparison. A focused medical update can be hundreds of times smaller than the original reading list and still meaningfully shift what the model knows.

03Where it fits

Where it lands in the training story.

It comes after
The base model has finished its first round of reading. You bolt on more reading from a domain or a fresher window of time.
Not the same as
Copying ideal answers. That step shapes how the model replies. This step changes what it has read.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • Your field has its own vocabulary that the original model barely saw (medicine, law, a niche programming language).
  • The model's knowledge stops a year or two before today and you need it more current.
  • You have a large pile of plain text but not enough question-and-answer pairs to do the heavier tuning steps.

Sources
  • Gururangan et al. (2020). Don't Stop Pretraining: Adapt Language Models to Domains and Tasks. arXiv:2004.10964. Shows that more reading on in-domain text reliably lifts results, even on top of strong base models.
