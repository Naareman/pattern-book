Compliance mapping

Read the rule, pull out the clauses that bite, and tie each clause to one control inside the system. Audits stop being a panic and start being a lookup.

01What is it?

What is it?

Compliance mapping translates each clause of a regulation (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2) into a specific control already built into the system, so an auditor's question becomes a row lookup rather than a panic.

02An example

Pick a regulation. See its clauses mapped.

A snippet from a real mapping document. Each line takes a clause and points to the control or evidence that satisfies it.

One clause, one control, one piece of evidence. The mapping is small on purpose: a long answer to a yes-or-no question is what triggers more questions.
03Where it fits

How it relates to nearby ideas.

What it covers: the bridge between a regulation and a running system. A document, owned by legal and security, kept fresh as the law changes and as the system ships.
Sits next to: the audit log is the evidence. The mapping is the index that says which log answers which clause. Together they let you answer an auditor in minutes instead of weeks.
Quick test: pick any clause from your top regulation. Can you point to the one control and the one piece of evidence that satisfies it, in under sixty seconds? If not, the mapping needs a refresh.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • You sell to enterprises that send security questionnaires before signing: the mapping is what fills them in.
  • You operate in a regulated sector: health, finance, education, public sector, child-facing products.
  • You ship in the European Union and need to track the new obligations the EU AI Act adds on top of GDPR.
