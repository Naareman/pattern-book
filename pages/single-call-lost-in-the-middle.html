Lost-in-the-Middle Mitigation

Models pay less attention to content buried in the middle of long prompts. This technique counters that by controlling where critical information lands.

01What It Is

The U-shaped attention problem.

Research shows that language models have a lost-in-the-middle bias: they attend more strongly to content at the beginning and end of a long prompt, and tend to under-use information in the middle. This is a structural property of current attention mechanisms, not a bug to be patched. Mitigation strategies work around it by positioning the most important information at the edges, repeating key instructions near the end, and breaking large documents into smaller labeled chunks so no single critical passage gets buried.

The critical clause is buried in document 3 of 5. The model gives it low attention and misses it in its answer.
Mitigation strategies
Position critical documents first or last in the context block, not in the middle of a stack.
Repeat key instructions near the query. A brief restatement at the end ("Remember: cite only the documents above") pulls attention back to the constraint.
Label every chunk clearly with a heading like [Document 3 of 5: Liability Clause] so the model can identify what it is reading as it processes the middle.
03Where It Fits

Its place in the taxonomy.

L4
Home layer
Input Shape sub-axis. All mitigation strategies are structural choices about how you arrange text in the prompt before the model call.
L4
Companion
Context Packing (also L4 Input Shape) is often where this problem first appears. Lost-in-the-Middle Mitigation is the discipline that makes packed prompts work reliably.
Filing rule: Rearranging and labeling content in the prompt is a text structuring decision made before the single call. It requires no second call or external system, so it stays in L4 Input Shape.
04When It Matters

Who needs this.

Anyone sending prompts over 10k tokens, especially in document Q&A, legal review, or code analysis. If users report that the model "ignored the relevant section" despite it being present in the prompt, lost-in-the-middle is the likely culprit. Apply this before reaching for a more expensive solution like fine-tuning or retrieval.
