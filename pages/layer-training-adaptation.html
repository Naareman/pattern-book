Training & adaptation

How the AI is taught and shaped before you ever talk to it.

01The Idea

What is it?

Before you send a single message, the AI goes through three rounds of school. First it reads an enormous pile of text from the internet to learn language (pretraining). Then it studies worked examples for a specific job (fine-tuning). Finally, humans rate its answers and it adjusts to match their preferences (RLHF). Each round builds on the last, making the answer a little better.

03A real example

GPT-4, stage by stage.

📚
Pretraining
GPT-4 trained on roughly a trillion words: books, Wikipedia, code, websites.
This gave it general knowledge and the ability to produce fluent text in many languages and styles.
🎯
Fine-tuning
OpenAI staff wrote thousands of ideal question-and-answer pairs and trained GPT-4 to copy that style.
This is what turned a next-word predictor into a conversational assistant that follows instructions.
👍
RLHF
Human raters compared pairs of answers and chose the better one. The model learned to predict those ratings, then optimised for them.
This is what made it helpful, honest, and less likely to say harmful things.
04Tradeoffs

What training can't fix.

Knowledge cutoff. Pretraining ends at a date. Anything after that date the model simply does not know, no matter how you prompt it.
Fine-tuning costs money. Retraining on new examples is expensive. Most teams only fine-tune once, then rely on prompting for the rest.
RLHF can over-optimise. If raters prefer confident-sounding answers, the model learns to sound confident even when it is wrong. The human signal is not perfect.
Training is not the last word. How you prompt the trained model matters just as much. A well-trained model still gives poor answers to poorly written questions.
