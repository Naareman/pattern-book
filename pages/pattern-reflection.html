Pattern

Reflection

The model writes a first answer, then reviews and critiques it, then produces an improved version. Three beats, one call.

01The Idea

What is it?

You ask the model to do its work twice. First it writes a draft. Then you ask it: "What is wrong with that answer? Now fix it." The model acts as its own critic. The second answer is usually sharper, more complete, and catches at least one thing the first pass missed.

02See It Work

Three beats, one fix.

Watch the model write a draft, spot its own flaw, then revise. Tap Next to step through, or Play to run all three.

1 First draft
To stay focused while studying, remove distractions and set a timer for 25 minutes.
↓
2 Self-critique
The draft mentions what to do but not why it works or what to do when focus breaks. It is too thin for someone who has already tried a timer and still struggles.
↓
3 Improved answer
To stay focused while studying, remove your phone from the room and set a 25-minute timer (the Pomodoro method). Your brain treats the end of the timer as a reward, which makes starting easier. If focus breaks mid-session, write down the distraction and return to it after the timer ends rather than acting on it immediately. After three sessions, take a 15-minute break to let working memory consolidate.
03When It Matters

Helps and limits.

Works well for
Writing and prose where completeness is hard to check in one pass
Code where the first version compiles but misses edge cases
Multi-part answers where one section is weaker than the rest
Known limits
The model can miss its own factual errors: the critique is confident but still wrong
It costs an extra round of tokens, so latency and price both go up
Shallow tasks (a short fact, a one-word answer) rarely benefit
Reflection vs. Chain-of-Thought. Chain-of-Thought is one forward reasoning pass: the model thinks through steps and lands on an answer. Reflection is a review-and-fix loop: the model finishes an answer, then reads it back and revises. They are different shapes. CoT improves reasoning on the way to an answer; Reflection improves an answer that already exists.
