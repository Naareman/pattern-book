Transformation

Keep the meaning, change the shape. Summaries, translations, and rewrites all live here.

01What is it?

Same meaning, different form.

The output carries the same content as the input rendered in a new shape: shorter, translated, reworded, or restructured.

02An Example

One paragraph, three forms.

Before
The Q3 board meeting covered revenue performance against plan, hiring pace in the Cairo office, the renewed cloud contract, and a proposed change to the bonus structure for engineering.
→
After
Q3 board covered revenue, hiring, cloud contract, and engineering bonus changes.
Before (English)
Please confirm your appointment for tomorrow at 10 a.m.
→
After (Arabic)
يُرجى تأكيد موعدك غدًا في العاشرة صباحًا.
Before (blunt)
Your invoice is overdue. Pay now or service stops.
→
After (warm)
A quick reminder that your invoice is past due. Settling it this week will keep your service running smoothly.
03Where it fits

How it sits next to other shapes.

Sits next to
Generation. Both produce text the user reads, but transformation is anchored to the input meaning while generation invents from a small seed.
Quality bar
Faithfulness: did the meaning survive the reshape? Summarization adds a second bar: coverage of the key points.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The source text is long, dense, or in the wrong language for the reader.
  • The same idea has to ship in several voices: an email, a tweet, a help-doc paragraph.
  • Editorial polish at scale: tone, length, reading level on every piece going out.
