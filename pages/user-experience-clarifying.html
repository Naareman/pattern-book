A follow-up question when it is unsure

When the request can be read two ways, the AI asks a short question back instead of guessing.

01What is it?

Asking back, not guessing wrong.

People often ask in shorthand. "Book me a flight to Paris" leaves the date, the city of origin, and the budget all open. A guess gets it wrong half the time. A clarifying question, asked only when it really matters, narrows the request before the AI commits. The person stays in the loop, and the answer lands closer to what they meant the first time.

02An example

Same request, two paths.

YouBook me a flight to Paris.
AIQuick check before I search: leaving from London, and any date in the next two weeks?
YouFrom Cairo, next Friday, one-way.
AIGot it. Here are three options for Friday from Cairo to Paris, one-way, sorted by price.

One short check turns a vague request into a clean answer.

YouBook me a flight to Paris.
AIBooked: round-trip from London to Paris, leaving tomorrow morning, returning Sunday. Confirmation sent.
YouWait, I am in Cairo and I needed one-way next Friday.
AICancelling now. Sorry about that.

No check means a wrong booking and a rollback.

04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • The request is short and could mean a few different things.
  • The cost of guessing wrong is real: money spent, a booking made, a message sent.
  • Trust matters more than speed: a small check shows the AI is paying attention.
