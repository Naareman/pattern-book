Learning from human ratings

Show people two model answers side by side, ask which one is better, and use those preferences to nudge the model toward the kind of reply humans actually like.

01What is it?

"Which answer is better?" turned into training signal.

The model writes two answers to the same question; a person picks the one they prefer; that vote is fed back into training so the model leans toward producing answers like the winner next time. The recipe was originally called "reinforcement learning from human feedback," and newer methods (such as direct preference optimization) reach the same goal with less machinery.

02An example

Pick the answer you would prefer.

User asks
I'm anxious about a job interview tomorrow. What can I do tonight?
Tap one. The model collects thousands of these little votes and learns the pattern: warm, direct, useful beats stiff and corporate.
03Where it fits

Where it sits in the training story.

It comes after
Copying ideal answers. You start from a model that already answers in roughly the right shape, then use ratings to shape taste, warmth, and refusals.
Not the same as
Showing the model the "right" answer outright. Here it never sees a perfect answer, only that one option beat another.
04When it matters

When to reach for it.

  • "Better" is easier to point at than to write out: tone, warmth, safety, taste.
  • You want the model to refuse the right requests and help with the others; ratings teach that line cleanly.
  • Two answers from the same model often differ in ways you can feel but cannot quite spec.

Sources
  • Christiano et al. (2017). Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences. arXiv:1706.03741. The foundational paper for training models using human side-by-side preferences.
  • Ouyang et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. arXiv:2203.02155. Applies the preference-rating recipe to language models, giving us the InstructGPT and ChatGPT line.
  • Rafailov et al. (2023). Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model. arXiv:2305.18290. A lighter recipe that learns from the same preference data with much less moving machinery.
