Tracking speed and cost

Record what every call did: which model, how long, how many tokens, which steps fired. That record is what lets you find the slow spots and the expensive ones.

01What Is It?

Leave a trail for every call.

The runtime emits a small record for each request: a trace. Each step (cache check, model call, tool call, retrieval) writes its time, its token count, its cost, and any errors. Later you can replay the trail to see why a call was slow, which model the router picked, and what the total bill was. The runtime only emits the signals. Deciding what to look at and what to fix is a separate discipline that lives in the observability crosscut.

03Where It Fits

How it sits next to other ideas.

What it shapes
Nothing about the answer itself. It only emits the data so someone (or another system) can see what happened: timing, cost, which step did what.
Feeds into
Cost tracking, latency reviews, and incident debugging. The numbers are also what the "pick the cheapest model" decision relies on later, since you cannot pick well without knowing what each tier actually costs and how often it suffices.
Mechanism vs discipline
The hooks (the code that emits the records) live here in the runtime. Tracing as a way of thinking (what to log, how to look at it, when to alert) is a separate crosscut concern.
04When It Matters

When to reach for it.

  • The system is in production and surprises start happening: a slow call, a spike in spend, a wrong answer you cannot reproduce.
  • Calls have many steps (retrieval, tools, multiple models) and "the AI was slow" is not enough to debug.
  • Cost is climbing and you cannot say which feature or which prompt is driving it.
  • You want to set budgets and alerts, which need real numbers from real traffic, not estimates.
